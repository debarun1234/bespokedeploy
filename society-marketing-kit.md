Two audiences, two angles, same underlying business. Swap in your actual society name, tower number, and link before using.

Pricing to keep straight across every script below: **Portfolio sites are ₹6,500** (individuals — students, professionals, freelancers). **Small Business sites are ₹12,000** (an actual business — cloud kitchen, tuition, salon, any home business with products/services/bookings). ## 3. The 15-second verbal pitch (lift, parking lot, society event)

> "Hey, I actually build websites for a living — BespokeDeploy.in. If [their business] doesn't have a proper site yet, I'd genuinely love to build you one. I only take 2 clients at a time so I'm not spread thin, and since we're neighbors you're not chasing some random freelancer if anything ever needs fixing. Want me to send you a few examples?"

Say it, don't sell it. The goal is to plant it casually enough that they bring it up again later — that's when they're actually ready.

---

## The psychology behind Part 1

**In-group trust.** People trust "one of us" far more than an outsider, even before any pitch is made — it's one of the strongest, best-documented biases in persuasion research. Every version leads with *neighbor*, not *freelancer*. "Tower [X]" is doing more work than any feature list could.

**Loss aversion, not feature-selling.** The opening line isn't "I build great websites" — it's "would someone find you if they looked?" People are far more moved by the fear of a customer they're already losing than by the promise of a new feature. Let them supply the anxiety; you're just naming it.

**Real scarcity, honestly framed.** You already cap at 2 projects — that's not a marketing trick, it's just true. Framing it as "opening this round to our society first" turns a real operational constraint into something that feels like an invitation, not a waitlist. Never manufacture urgency you don't have — but you don't need to here, so use the real thing.

**Proximity kills their biggest fear.** The number one hesitation with any freelancer is "what if they vanish after I pay." You living in the building is the single strongest answer to that objection you could possibly have, and it costs you nothing to say. Say it explicitly every time — don't assume it's obvious.

**Reciprocity, softly.** Offering to "send a few examples" for free, no pressure, is a small give that primes them to reciprocate later. It also lets your actual work (Sheetal's site, Wound Care by Axcess) do the selling instead of your words.

**Identity validation.** People running a cloud kitchen out of their kitchen or tuition classes out of their living room are proud of what they built, often quietly so — nobody's telling them it's a "real business." Naming that ("the work you put into it," "your business deserves to look as serious as you are") lands better than any technical pitch about SEO or hosting.

# Part 2 — Professionals & Students: Portfolio Sites (₹6,500 tier)

Different audience, different anxiety. Home-business owners worry about being found; this crowd worries about being *skipped* — a recruiter, a client, a professor scrolling past them in seconds. The pitch should feel less "neighbor helping neighbor" and more "insider tip that gives you an edge."

## 1. LinkedIn / Instagram post

> Recruiters spend about 6 seconds on a candidate before deciding to keep reading or move on. A Canva link or a plain PDF resume doesn't survive those 6 seconds. A real website does.
>
> I build portfolio websites for students and professionals — ₹6,500, one-time, hosting free forever. No monthly fees eating into your pocket money or your first salary. Built in 5-7 days, and it's genuinely yours: your domain, your case studies, your projects, laid out properly instead of crammed into a resume template.
>
> If you're heading into placements, applying for internships, or just tired of your "portfolio" being a Google Drive link — let's talk. BespokeDeploy.in
>
> [your link]

> To everyone prepping for placements or internships this season —
>
> Quick honest question: when a recruiter or company Googles you, what comes up? If the answer is "nothing" or "just LinkedIn," you're leaving an easy edge on the table.
>
> I build proper portfolio websites — not a template you wrestle with, an actual site built around your projects and story. ₹6,500 one-time (no subscriptions, ever), live in about a week. A few examples if you want to see the quality first: [portfolio link]

## 3. The 10-second pitch (campus, coffee shop, group project chats)

> "Does your portfolio actually look like something, or is it a Notion page you're hoping nobody clicks too hard on? I build real portfolio sites — one-time cost, no subscription, looks like you actually take this seriously. Want to see a couple I've built?"

---

## The psychology behind Part 2

**Fear of being skipped, not fear of being invisible.** Home-business owners fear nobody finds them at all; students and professionals fear being found and then *dismissed* in seconds. "6 seconds" and "skipped past" name that specific anxiety — it's sharper and more true to how hiring and networking actually feel than a generic "stand out online" line.

**Subscription aversion is the strongest lever with this audience.** Students in particular have been burned by "free trial then ₹500/month forever" tools their whole digital life. Hammering "one-time, ₹6,500, no subscription, ever" does more conversion work here than almost anything else in the pitch — say it in every version, not just once.

**Borrowed authority through your own examples.** This audience is naturally skeptical of anyone selling them something during a stressful, high-stakes season (placements, job hunting). Showing your own portfolio and past client sites up front — before they even ask — pre-empts the "wait, are you legit" question instead of making them dig for it.

**Real, not fake, urgency.** Placement season and application deadlines are genuine time pressure already sitting in their head — you don't need to invent scarcity here, just point at the calendar they're already anxious about ("before applications pick up").

**Peer signaling.** Nobody wants to be the one classmate still sending a PDF resume while others show up with a real site. This works quietly in the background of every version — you don't need to say "your friends will have this," the fear of falling behind peers is already doing that work on its own.

---

## Practical rollout order

**Part 1 (society):** flyer in lift lobbies and notice board first, then the WhatsApp broadcast a few evenings later, then the verbal pitch opportunistically for weeks after — that last one is usually what actually closes it.

**Part 2 (students/professionals):** the LinkedIn/Instagram post works best right as a placement or internship cycle is starting to buzz — timing it to that anxiety matters more than the copy itself. Campus WhatsApp groups convert faster than social posts because it reaches people already worried about this specific thing, not scrolling casually.
